## What makes them work (the rules)

**Story**
1. **The joke is the consequence, not the growth.** Every step shows *one specific thing that happens because of the number* — a real-world benchmark
   ("office chairs are tested for 125 kg", "194 dB is the limit for sound in air", "escape velocity") paid off by a visible event (the chair collapses).
2. **Two-beat captions:** a factual setup line, then a deadpan punchline ("Day 69. It's now as long as you are tall." → "Nice.").
   Bureaucratic understatement against catastrophe is the core joke style ("The police ask you to keep it down." · "The UN asks you to please stop." · "HR fires you for 'safety reasons'.").
3. **Skip steps.** Only show the numbers that have a great consequence (Day 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 12, 14, 18, 22…; kick #1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11…). Never grind through every doubling.
4. **The thing that escalates varies:** doubling per day / hour / kick / burp, linear growth (+1 inch a day), or no growth at all (a world-reaction montage).
5. **Running gags + callbacks:** the queue of opponents ("The dojo is gone. The line isn't."), "excuse you" coming back from Mom at 340 dB.
6. **A pause beat mid-way:** "He's starting to enjoy this." / "Kick #40 is charging…" — a held face close-up breaks the rhythm before the biggest hit.
7. **Twist / poignant ending, often a loop:** "In space, no one can hear you burp." · "You're 107. Still single. Turns out size isn't everything." · loop back to shot 1.
8. **Length 50–120 s, ~2–4 s per step**, fast and relentless.

**Sound**
9. **BOMBOCLAT is not on every step.** It is a reaction used where it's funny, and **it varies every time**: "bombo… bombo… bomboclat!", "bomboclat?", "hmm… bomboclat",
   a long "BOMBOOOOOOO…" cut off by the next impact, a whispered one, a stuttered one. Some reels have **no shout at all** (music + captions only).
10. Music bed + punchy SFX (impacts, shatters, whooshes, sonic booms); dialogue only when the story is a two-character conversation (cigarettes).

**Visuals (the quality bar)**
11. **Cinematic lighting:** warm practical lights (lamps, neon, a glowing cigarette tip), rim light, volumetric smoke/haze, bokeh lights in the background,
    shallow depth of field, a filmic grade (warm/teal). Night scenes with emissive windows and screens.
12. **Camera:** extreme face close-ups for reactions, macro inserts of props (scale display, ashtray, clock), low hero angles, slow push-ins,
    big aerial wides of dense cities, a camera that rides with the action (following the kicked opponent through walls).
13. **Physical destruction you can read:** planks, glass, dust clouds, shockwave rings, cracked glowing crust, debris arcs, speed streaks.
14. **Big set pieces at scale:** dense instanced cities, Earth from orbit with clouds and city lights, rings, black hole with an accretion disk.
15. **Text:** the same stat block we use + a 1–2 line italic diary caption directly under it (Lora italic), and a status pill that escalates (CASUAL → CHIMNEY).
